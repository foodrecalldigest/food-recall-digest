## What it is

Official recall notices are scattered across several agencies, written for industry, and often leave out the one thing shoppers care about: **where the product was sold**. Food Recall Digest pulls them together every morning and turns them into one easy-to-scan page:

- **Every new recall** from the FDA and the USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service (FSIS), ranked by risk:
  🔴 **High risk** (bacteria, toxins, illnesses reported) · 🟠 **Medium** (foreign objects, lead, uninspected or improperly imported products) · 🔵 **Allergen** · ⚪ **Low**
- **Exactly what to look for** — every product, size, UPC, lot code and date, never "and more".
- **Where it was sold** — stores named in official notices and USDA retail distribution lists, with tap-to-open map links.
- **What to do** — a plain-language action for each recall.
- **Still-active outbreaks** — the latest FDA/CDC illness counts for ongoing investigations.
- **Product photos** from the official FDA notices.

Each digest is published here and emailed to subscribers — **only on days when something is new or has changed.**

## How accuracy is handled

- Every fact (illness counts, states, UPCs, lot codes, dates, store names) is re-checked against the official FDA, USDA FSIS or CDC page before publishing, and dated with the agency's "as of" date.
- Store information is labeled:
  - **✓ Confirmed** — named in the official notice or a USDA retail distribution list.
  - **Lead** — a business known to sell the brand, **not** confirmed to have sold the recalled lots.
  - **Not named** — the agency or company has not released store names.
- Nothing is invented: if a detail is not in an official source, it says "not stated".

## Important

**This is an independent summary of public government data. It is not an official FDA, USDA or CDC notice and is not affiliated with any government agency.** Always confirm details on the official notice linked in each recall.
