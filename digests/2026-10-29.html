Elevated lead. Retail and grocery stores in 14 states.

Show details

Wise Wife Brand Ground Cinnamon, 110 g (3.88 oz) (SLR Food Distribution Inc.)

What’s wrong

  • SLR Food Distribution Inc. of Hicksville, NY, is recalling Wise Wife Brand Ground Cinnamon.
  • It may have elevated levels of lead.
  • A sample tested by The Connecticut Agricultural Experiment Station found elevated lead.
  • The cinnamon was sold in retail and grocery stores in CT, IN, KS, MA, MI, MN, NC, NE, NY, OH, PA, TX, VA and WI from Sep 8, 2025 to Jun 28, 2026.
  • Short-term exposure to very low levels of lead may not cause any symptoms.
  • A child exposed over a long time may suffer permanent damage to the central nervous system.
  • No illnesses have been reported to date.

Check your kitchen

●  Germs & toxinsFDA · Oct 27

Possible botulism (Clostridium botulinum). Stores, seafood counters and restaurants in CT, NJ, NY, PA.

Do this: Don't eat them.
Show details

Cold smoked sprats, all lots, sold loose in bulk boxes (D&M Smoked Fish Inc., doing business as Haifa Smoked Fish Inc.)

What’s wrong

  • Haifa Smoked Fish of Queens, NY, is recalling about 560 lbs of cold smoked sprats.
  • They may be contaminated with Clostridium botulinum, a bacterium that can cause life-threatening illness.
  • An FDA inspection found the firm made cold-smoked sprats without the required food safety controls to prevent the germ's growth and toxin formation.
  • FDA warned people not to eat, sell or serve the sprats on Oct 23.
  • At that time FDA said the firm had declined its recall request.
  • The company announced this recall on Oct 27.
  • Symptoms of botulism include general weakness, dizziness, double vision and trouble speaking or swallowing.
  • Trouble breathing is also possible.
  • Botulism is a medical emergency.
  • No illnesses have been reported to date.

Check your kitchen

  • Cold Smoked Sprats, all lots, no brand name
  • Packed loose in bulk cardboard boxes labeled "Cold Smoked Sprats" and shipped refrigerated
  • Shipping labels may read "302M, LOT# 261, C/Smoked Holland Sprats"
  • May have been sold at grocery seafood counters or restaurants, with or without a label
  • May have been kept frozen for up to one year

Where it was sold

Stores not named in the notice
  • Sold through retail stores in New Jersey, New York and Pennsylvania (company notice).
  • FDA's advisory adds Connecticut, says the sprats went to retailers, restaurants, wholesalers and food service customers, and says they may have reached other states.
  • No store names are given.
Do this:
  • Don't eat them.
  • Return them to the place of purchase for a full refund.
  • FDA says: "If you can't tell if your sprats are from Haifa Smoked Fish, Inc., do not eat them and throw them away." Clean and sanitize any surfaces or containers that touched them.
  • Anyone with symptoms should get medical care right away.
  • Questions: Haifa Smoked Fish, 718‑523‑8899, 9 AM to 5 PM ET.
